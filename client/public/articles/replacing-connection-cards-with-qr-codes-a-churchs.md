## Introduction

For many churches, connection cards have been a staple for gathering visitor information and encouraging engagement. However, in an increasingly digital world, these paper forms can feel outdated. Many visitors today prefer the convenience of technology, and QR codes present a powerful solution for churches looking to modernize their approach to connection.

In this article, we’ll explore how churches can replace traditional connection cards with QR codes, highlighting the benefits of automated digital workflows and providing practical steps to implement this change.

## Why Switch to QR Codes?

### 1. Visitor Preference
Studies consistently show that people prefer quick and easy methods to share their information. QR codes allow visitors to engage without the hassle of filling out paper forms. A recent survey indicated that over 70% of respondents found digital forms simpler and less time-consuming than traditional methods.

### 2. Streamlined Processes
Switching to QR codes can significantly streamline the connection process:
- **Immediate Data Capture**: Information is captured in real-time, reducing the risk of lost cards or misentered data.
- **Automated Follow-up**: With the right tools, you can automate follow-up emails or messages, enhancing the visitor experience.

### 3. Customizable User Experience
QR codes can link to tailored mobile journeys. For example, you can create flows that guide visitors through different options, such as:
- Signing up for newsletters
- Donating to the church
- Joining small groups

## How to Implement QR Codes in Your Church

### Step 1: Choose a QR Code Platform
Select a platform that allows you to create custom QR codes and automate workflows. GiveLive is a robust option that provides various templates to simplify this process. Here are some templates that might be useful:
- [New Member](/blog/new-member): Welcome new visitors and connect with them.
- [Small Groups](/blog/small-groups): Connect people to small groups.
- [Event Check-in](/blog/event-checkin): Streamline event registration and check-ins.

### Step 2: Set Up Your QR Flows
Using your chosen platform, create a QR flow that aligns with your goals. Here’s a quick outline to help you get started:
1. **Identify Objectives**: Define what you want to achieve—lead capture, donations, event registration, etc.
2. **Select a Template**: Use one of GiveLive’s templates that matches your needs.
3. **Customize the Flow**: Tailor the lead capture form to gather essential information (name, email, etc.) and any additional details that might be helpful.
4. **Integrate with Your CRM**: Ensure your data flows directly into your CRM (like HubSpot or Salesforce) for easy management and follow-up.

### Step 3: Create Your QR Code
Once your flow is set up, generate your QR code. Most platforms will allow you to customize the appearance of the QR code, so it aligns with your church’s branding. 

### Step 4: Promote Your QR Code
Make your QR code visible and accessible:
- **Print on Bulletins**: Include the QR code on weekly bulletins or newsletters.
- **Post on Social Media**: Share the QR code on your church’s social media platforms.
- **Display at Events**: Have signs with the QR code at events, so visitors can easily scan and connect.

### Step 5: Analyze and Optimize
After implementing your QR code system:
- **Track Engagement**: Use analytics tools provided by your QR code platform to monitor how many scans and submissions you receive.
- **Adjust Based on Feedback**: Solicit feedback from visitors regarding their experience and make adjustments to improve the process.

## Benefits of Digital Connection Cards
Switching to QR codes not only simplifies the process for visitors but also offers several additional benefits for your church:
- **Cost-Effective**: Eliminates the need for printing and supplies associated with paper forms.
- **Environmentally Friendly**: Reduces paper waste, aligning with sustainability efforts many congregations value.
- **Flexibility**: Easily modify your flow or update information without the need for new print materials.
